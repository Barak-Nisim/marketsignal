Methodology

How MarketSignal works

Every step below is real: the same code that renders this page pulls the market data and runs the scoring engine. Nothing here is a marketing simplification of something different happening under the hood.

Pull real financial data

A US ticker is looked up against Yahoo Finance for current fundamentals (valuation ratios, margins, growth, debt) and a year of price history. Not every ticker publishes every metric; ETFs in particular usually have no income-statement data at all, since they aren't operating companies.

Score five categories against fixed thresholds

Valuation, Growth, Profitability, Financial Health, and Momentum are each a weighted average of metric scores, on a 0–4 scale from Weak to Strong. Every threshold is documented and recomputable by hand; there is no machine-learned model deciding the number.

Missing data is excluded, not penalized

A missing P/E ratio doesn't mean a company is doing poorly; it usually means the metric doesn't apply (negative earnings, or an ETF with no earnings at all). Missing metrics are excluded from their category's average rather than scored as zero. If an entire category has no available data, that category is excluded from the overall score too.

AI narrates. It never re-scores, and it never says Buy or Sell.

With AI narration on, Claude receives the already-computed scores as structured data and is explicitly instructed to write a reasoned thesis and confidence level, not to recompute the numbers or issue a trade recommendation. The scoring engine works, and is fully tested, with zero dependency on any AI service.

What changed since last time

Every run is saved locally (outside the codebase entirely, since it's real personal research data). The next run against the same ticker shows the delta against your last look, so you're reading what moved, not re-reading the whole picture from scratch.

Want the exact thresholds for every metric? It's documented in the repo: scoring methodology and architecture.
